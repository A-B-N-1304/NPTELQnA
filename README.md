# NPTELQnA
With the rapid expansion of digital education platforms, long-form lecture videos have become a prevalent medium for delivering academic content at scale. Platforms such as the National Programme on Technology Enhanced Learning (NPTEL) host thousands of hours of expert-led instruction across disciplines, offering valuable educational resources to a wide audience. However, despite their richness, these videos are inherently static and non-interactive, often limiting students’ ability to engage actively with the material. Unlike traditional classroom settings where learners can pose questions and receive immediate clarifications, video lectures present information in a linear format, requiring students to independently navigate, interpret, and review content to resolve doubts.
Recent advancements in natural language processing (NLP), particularly in the domain of transformer-based language models, have significantly improved the capabilities of question-answering (QA) systems. Pretrained models such as T5, BERT, and GPT have demonstrated remarkable performance on a variety of open-domain QA tasks, benefiting from large-scale training on diverse datasets. However, these general-purpose models often fall short when applied to domain-specific contexts, such as academic discourse, where terminology, instructional style, and conceptual explanations differ markedly from general text corpora.
Efforts to bridge this gap have included domain adaptation through fine-tuning, as well as the development of specialized QA datasets in fields such as biomedicine, law, and education. In the educational domain, previous research has explored question answering over textbooks, lecture slides, and even multimodal content, yet the application of generative QA techniques to long-form lecture videos remains underexplored.
Furthermore, while extractive QA approaches can locate relevant spans in text, they often lack the ability to generate natural, explanatory responses. Generative QA systems, by contrast, can synthesize complete answers that align more closely with pedagogical intent, making them well-suited for educational applications.
This research builds on these developments by proposing a two-stage generative QA framework that combines large-scale pretraining with domain-specific fine-tuning on NPTEL lecture transcripts. The goal is to create an intelligent educational assistant capable of answering students’ questions in a way that mimics the explanatory clarity and contextual relevance of a human instructor. By enabling semantic understanding and natural response generation from video content, this system aims to enhance student engagement, support self-paced learning, and ultimately make online education more interactive and accessible.
